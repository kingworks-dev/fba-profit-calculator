Worked example

A product costs $6.00 to make and sells for $24.99. Amazon takes a 15% referral fee ($3.75), the FBA fee is $5.40, inbound shipping is $0.80 and storage is $0.10 per unit per month.

Total cost per unit is $16.05, so net profit is $8.94, a margin of about 35.8% and an ROI of about 131.5% on the $6.80 invested (product plus inbound shipping). At 300 units a month that is roughly $2,682 in monthly net profit before advertising.

The break-even price is (6.00 + 5.40 + 0.80 + 0.10) / (1 − 0.15) = $14.47. Any price above that makes money; any price below loses it.

FBA profit questions

How do you calculate Amazon FBA profit?

Start with the sale price and subtract the product cost, the Amazon referral fee (a percentage of the sale price, commonly 15%), the FBA fulfillment fee, inbound shipping per unit and monthly storage per unit. What remains is net profit per unit. Margin is profit divided by sale price. ROI is profit divided by what you paid up front, here product cost plus inbound shipping.

What is a good FBA profit margin?

Many sellers aim for a net margin of 20-30% or more and an ROI above 50-100% before advertising. Margins under roughly 15% leave little room for ad spend, returns and price changes. This calculator does not include advertising, so subtract your expected cost per sale.

What fees does Amazon charge FBA sellers?

The main fees are the referral fee (usually 8-15% of the sale price depending on category), the FBA fulfillment fee based on size and weight, and monthly inventory storage. Long-term storage, removals and returns processing can add more. Check Amazon's current fee schedule for exact rates for your category.

How is the break-even price calculated?

Add every fixed per-unit cost (product, FBA fee, inbound shipping, storage) and divide by one minus the referral rate. Selling at that price covers all costs with zero profit left over.
